"Avoiding your triggers isn't healing. Healing happens when you're triggered and you're able to move through the pain, the pattern, and the story — and walk your way to a different ending."
Vienna Pharaon — A guiding philosophy at A Nu Day

To most people, "licensed therapist" and "pole dance instructor" don't belong in the same sentence. Adrianne has spent her career proving otherwise.

The truth is, both worlds are rooted in the same belief: that people have the right to heal on their own terms, in their own bodies, in their own way. Mental health work and movement aren't opposites — they're partners.

Adrianne's background in the creative and performing arts shaped her long before she became a clinician. She knows what it means to use a body as a vehicle for expression, release, and transformation. That knowledge lives in every therapy session and every class she teaches.

The Therapist

Trained to Hold
the Hardest Stories

Adrianne earned her Master of Social Work from Washington University in St. Louis — one of the top social work programs in the country. She built her clinical career working in crisis care facilities, psychiatric units, residential settings, and suicide prevention hotlines.

She specializes in people who are navigating thoughts of suicide and self-harm — a population many clinicians find difficult to serve. For Adrianne, it's a calling. She uses Dialectical Behavior Therapy groups, individual counseling, couples therapy, and expressive arts techniques to meet clients where they are and help them build skills to stay.

She also trains other professionals — teaching Applied Suicide Intervention Skills, crisis response, and trauma-informed care to teams and organizations who want to be better equipped for the hardest moments.

The Instructor

Reclaiming Power

Free Flow Fitness isn't just a studio — it's a philosophy made physical. Adrianne built it as a space where women of every shape, size, background, and athletic ability can walk in uncertain and leave transformed.

The Intersection

Why the Body Matters
in the Healing Room

Adrianne holds advanced training in Dance/Movement Therapy and Expressive Arts & Integrative Therapies — certifications that formalize what she already knew intuitively: the body holds what the mind can't always say out loud.

Trauma lives in the nervous system. Shame lives in posture. Grief lives in breath. And sometimes the most direct path to healing is through movement — not around it. That's why Adrianne integrates body-based techniques directly into her clinical practice, not as an add-on, but as a core modality.

She is a member of both the American Association of Suicidology and the American Dance Therapy Association — two professional worlds that rarely overlap, held together by one person who refuses to choose between them.

Her Approach

The Belief Behind
All of It

Adrianne's work — in the therapy room and in the studio — is built on a single conviction: that every person has the right to define their own healing and inhabit their own body, fully and on their own terms. She calls this "Nu" — the statistical symbol for degrees of freedom.

🧠

No Cookie-Cutter Therapy

There is no one-size-fits-all approach to mental health. Adrianne blends DBT, CBT, Emotionally Focused Therapy, ACT, Motivational Interviewing, and expressive arts to find the combination that works for each individual — not a formula, but a conversation.

💃

The Body Is Not Separate

Western mental health has long tried to treat the mind as if it floats above the body. Adrianne rejects that. She teaches her clients to re-enter their bodies as acts of healing — and she teaches her studio students to re-enter their bodies as acts of power. Both are medicine.

✦

Transparency Is Strength

Adrianne is transparent about who she is — publicly and unapologetically. She is an LCSW and a pole dance instructor. She believes hiding parts of yourself is the opposite of what she teaches clients to do. The full picture of who she is makes her more effective, not less credible.
